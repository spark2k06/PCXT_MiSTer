The XT family PC/XT · Tandy 1000 · PCjrin one FPGA core

One MiSTer core for the 8088 generation of the PC: the IBM PC/XT with CGA, HGC/MDA or EGA, the Tandy 1000 and the IBM PCjr. They share the MCL86 CPU, the KFPC-XT chipset and one memory system. The machine is chosen in the OSD and built at the next reset.

Overview

One core for the whole XT family

The PC/XT, the Tandy 1000 and the PCjr were separate MiSTer cores, each with its own copy of the CPU, chipset and video. Here they are one design: the hardware they share is written once, and what makes each machine different is switched in at reset. The RTL implements the machines themselves; MiSTer supplies storage, physical video and audio, controls and the menu.

XT

IBM PC/XT

8088 or 8086 at 4.77, 7.16 or 9.54 MHz, or unthrottled. CGA and HGC/MDA together, or an IBM EGA card. EMS, UMBs, XTIDE, AdLib, C/MS and Sound Blaster Pro.

TDY

Tandy 1000

CGA with the Tandy 16-colour modes, its video memory taken from system RAM. SN76489 sound, the Tandy keyboard and the Tandy BIOS in its own slot.

JR

IBM PCjr

The video gate array, the infrared keyboard, two cartridge slots, a diskette adapter driven by programmed I/O, 128–640 KiB of RAM and the tape sound line.

How to read this project

The machine decides which parts of the shared hardware exist. A Tandy 1000 has only its CGA, with no HGC or EGA. A PCjr has no expansion slots, no turbo, no hard disk and no second drive. The OSD hides what does not apply. The core also forces those options off, so a saved setting or a runtime-control port cannot bring them back.

Applied at reset

Changing the machine rebuilds the address map, the video path, the keyboard and the interrupt wiring, so these settings are latched only at reset. Until Reset & apply settings is chosen, the OSD shows that a change is pending. Options that are safe to change at run time, such as Fake 286 FLAGS or the audio levels, take effect at once.

On a PCjr, the core swaps COM1 and COM2. It also drives timer channel 1 from channel 0 when the BIOS asks for it, and makes the BIOS do a cold start after every reset: the SDRAM keeps the warm-start flag, so the core hides it until the BIOS writes it again.

MGL profiles

A MiSTer MGL file starts the core under a name of its own, and each name has its own config/<name>.CFG. With same_dir="1", every profile keeps using games/PCXT. So the same RBF can appear in the menu as PCXT, PCXT-Tandy, PCXT-PCjr and PCXT-EGA, each with its machine, BIOS and disks.

This is a feature of MiSTer's Main and needs no build of its own. The build profiles in section 08 are something else: separate RBFs with only part of the hardware.

03

Logical design

Core architecture

The CPU and the chipset form the XT bus. Memory, ROMs and most video memory sit in one SDRAM behind an arbiter. The video cards run in their own clock domains, and the MiSTer top level joins everything to the HPS, the OSD, DDRAM and the physical outputs.

Clock domains

The chipset and the CPU run from a 50 MHz clock, and the CPU speeds are counted against it. A second PLL takes that clock as its reference, so the video keeps a fixed phase with the CPU; cycle-locked effects depend on that. It produces:

  • 28.636 MHz: the CGA family, twice the 14.31818 MHz XT crystal.
  • 57.272 MHz: the video mixer, the EGA front end and the shared 350-line framebuffer.
  • 114.545 MHz: the SDRAM controller and the HGC/MDA front end.

Video I/O writes are stretched and acknowledged in the video clock, so short Max-speed bus cycles never lose a CRTC or mode-register write.

One memory system

The SDRAM holds the 1 MiB image of the CPU address space, a ROM bank with every BIOS slot and both cartridges, the video memory of the EGA, CGA and HGC/MDA cards, and the 2 MiB of EMS. Four clients share it: video fetch, the CPU bus, the ROM loader and a spare port.

Video requests carry a deadline and go first when it is close. Refreshes are owed every 7.8 µs, and up to eight can be postponed while the memory is busy. The CPU therefore finds at most one operation in front of it. In 8086 mode, word accesses take a private 16-bit path beside the 8-bit XT bus.

!
Native video and output conversion are separate

Everything software can see stays in the chipset and the video cards. Converting a picture for the physical output happens after the native raster and never feeds back into CRTC state, video memory or DOS-visible timing.

04

Colour video

The CGA family: CGA, Tandy 1000 and PCjr

The three machines use one CGA path: a 6845-style CRTC (the UM6845R type), the sequencer, attribute decoding, RGBI output and a composite decoder. The Tandy 1000 and the PCjr add their own registers and modes to it, and fetch their video memory from system RAM instead of from the card.

IBM CGA

  • 16 KiB of video memory at B8000h, mirrored to BFFFFh.
  • Text modes, and 320×200 and 640×200 graphics.
  • RGBI output with a selectable monochrome tint: green, amber, B&W and others.
  • Simulated NTSC composite colour, decoded from the RGBI transitions and the colour burst phase.
  • Visible border, and the 3DAh status with display enable and vertical retrace.

Tandy 1000 and PCjr

  • 160×200 and 320×200 in 16 colours, and 640×200 in 4 colours.
  • Tandy: the 3DEh palette and border registers, and port A0h picks the 128 KiB block of RAM that holds the picture.
  • PCjr: the gate array's 16 mode and palette registers, written through 3DAh, with its own status bits and a vertical-retrace interrupt.
  • The 3DFh page register sets the CPU and CRT pages, 16 or 32 KiB.
  • The picture is CPU RAM: there is no copy of it on a card.

CRTC frame stacking

Some programs make the 6845 start a new frame on every scanline. They program a tiny frame (R4 at 0 or 1, R9 at 0) and rewrite R0–R2 in the middle of each line, so every line gets its own start address. The water reflection in the end credits of Area 5150 is one example. Each of those writes must land within a few characters of its slot. Native runs the trick on the CRTC as it is, which needs CGA-exact CPU timing that the core does not reach.

Rebuilt keeps the registers as they are written. It records the start addresses in the order they are written and shows them on a stable 80-column raster of its own, so the picture depends on the order of the writes, not on their timing. It only takes over while R0 is rewritten on every line. A bench replays a real 5150 bus capture of that effect, with and without timing errors.

The graphical boot splash

The splash is a 320×200, 16-colour RGBI picture, stored two pixels per byte in splash_rom.v, with one read port for each renderer. On the CGA family, cga_splash_renderer.v holds each pixel for four 28.636 MHz clocks of the native aperture and sends it through the normal CGA output. Composite mode therefore sees the real colour transitions and carrier phase. With the EGA card, the EGA renderer draws it. The picture fades in and out, and Splash time shows it for 5, 10 or 15 seconds, or not at all. With HGC/MDA as the first video, the CGA draws the splash and the HGC/MDA output returns afterwards.

05

Monochrome and enhanced video

HGC/MDA and the EGA card

On a PC/XT, the CGA can work beside a Hercules-compatible monochrome card, or both give way to an IBM EGA. Their 350-line modes are not a 15 kHz picture, so a shared framebuffer can convert them for a television without changing the card that DOS sees.

HGC/MDA

  • Pages at B0000h and B8000h, selected by the card.
  • 720-dot, 350-line source timing.
  • Monochrome text with normal and bright intensity.
  • Hercules 720×348 graphics.
  • Live beside CGA: PCXT 1st Video tells the BIOS which card is first, and F11 swaps the MiSTer output between the two.

EGA, strictly

  • An IBM EGA: modes 0–7, 0Dh, 0Eh, 0Fh and 10h.
  • 64-colour attribute palette, pel panning and line compare.
  • 5151, 5153 and 5154 monitors, with the switch sense the BIOS reads.
  • 256 KiB of video memory in SDRAM. The display side is retimed so that the picture matches a BRAM card dot for dot, and a bench checks this.
  • The IBM EGA BIOS at C0000h, from its own OSD slot. There are no VGA modes.

350-line modes on a 15 kHz television

The converter, in vendor/pc_common (ega_fb_capture, ega_fb_readout, ega_ddr_arbiter), captures one complete frame in DDRAM and reads it out on a 910-dot raster of about 15.734 kHz. A frame is published only when it is complete and has the right geometry, and it is never replaced halfway through its two fields. 480i keeps every source line across two interlaced fields. 240p is progressive and steady, at the cost of vertical detail. The CGA family never enters this path.

06

System capabilities

The machine around the video

The options below are those of the default mister_pcxt_full build, the one published in releases/. Each is available on the machines that had it.

Processor and timing

  • 8088 mode with a four-byte prefetch queue.
  • 8086 mode with a six-byte queue and a private 16-bit SDRAM path.
  • Cycle-counted 4.77, 7.16 and 9.54 MHz settings.
  • Max, an unthrottled profile for throughput.
  • Fake 286 FLAGS, for software that probes the reserved FLAGS bits.

Memory and storage

  • 640 KiB of conventional memory; 128–640 KiB on the PCjr.
  • Lo-tech EMS up to 2 MiB, with its frame at D000h.
  • UMBs at A0000h (with the CGA/HGC card) and in the upper memory that no ROM or EMS frame takes.
  • Floppy A/B, two IDE images and the second SD; XTIDE at EC000h.
  • A real-time clock for DOS.

Audio

  • AdLib OPL2 (JTOPL) at 388h, or also at the Sound Blaster FM address 228h.
  • C/MS (SAA1099) or a Sound Blaster Pro at 220h, one at a time, because they collide on 226h/227h. The DSP reports version 3.00 (Pro 1) and uses IRQ 5 or 7.
  • SN76489 (JT89) on the Tandy 1000 and the PCjr, optional on the PC/XT.
  • PC speaker, PCjr tape sound, and MIDI through an MPU-401 UART or MT32-pi.

Input and expansion

  • PS/2 keyboard, sent as XT codes, Tandy codes or PCjr infrared frames.
  • Serial mouse on COM1, with a DOS driver such as CTMOUSE.
  • Two analog or digital joysticks, optionally synchronised to the CPU speed.
  • MPU-401 at 330h–331h, and USER I/O as MIDI or COM2.
  • Runtime-control ports at 8980h–898Fh (XTEGACTL), so a DOS launcher can set speed, sound and input without the OSD.

Address space

The 1 MiB memory map

The CPU sees a 20-bit address space. The same regions mean different things on each machine, and the selected PC/XT video card, the EMS and the UMBs change what answers in upper memory.

The map is an address-space view. It does not mean that every block is available to DOS as a UMB at the same time: the selected video card owns its aperture, EMS takes its 16 KiB banks, and XTIDE takes the EC00h window when it is loaded.

Getting started

BIOS, splash and startup

Recommended path

  1. Copy games/PCXT to the MiSTer SD card.
  2. Select Computers / PCXT and open the OSD with Win + F12.
  3. Choose the Machine. For a PC/XT, also choose the video card, the CPU type and the speed.
  4. Mount a floppy or hard-disk image.
  5. Load the BIOS of that machine under System & BIOS. The BIOS Builder prepares every image.
  6. Use Reset & apply settings.

ROM roles

  • The PCXT, Tandy and PCjr BIOS slots each fill F0000h–FFFFFh when their machine is chosen.
  • The EGA card needs an IBM EGA BIOS, at C0000h.
  • EC00 takes an XTIDE option ROM of up to 16 KiB. Some system BIOS images already carry XTIDE.
  • The PCjr takes two .jrc cartridges. Loading one restarts the machine, so the BIOS finds it.
  • The CGA, Tandy, PCjr and HGC/MDA video need no ROM of their own.
!
A missing BIOS is a deliberate hold

If the BIOS of the chosen machine, or the EGA BIOS, is absent, the core keeps the splash on screen and holds the machine in reset. The OSD names the missing slot. The CPU never runs from an empty F0000h, and the menu stays usable from whichever display is connected.

F12 behaviour

F12 pauses the splash countdown and shows the JTFrame credits while the machine stays held; pressing it again resumes the splash. After startup, the same key pauses the machine and shows the credits. Win + F12 opens the MiSTer OSD.

Verification and boundaries

What is checked and what remains deliberate

Every RTL directory has a TESTBENCH/ folder of focused benches, run locally with Icarus or Verilator. They check the contracts that are easiest to break: Max-speed bus cycles, clock crossings, the machine-specific glue and the video timing.

AreaBenchesWhat they hold down

rtl/mem12Memory maps, the SDRAM arbiter and controller, the ROM loader, video fetch and an end-to-end CPU path.

Known boundaries

  • Max is an unthrottled throughput profile, not historical hardware.
  • CRTC tricks timed to the exact CGA cycle need Rebuilt, not Native.
  • 240p trades vertical detail for a steady television picture.
  • Native HGC/MDA and EGA 350-line output need a display or scaler that accepts it.
  • BIOS and disk-image compatibility vary with the firmware and the media geometry.

The important boundary is explicit

Emulated features stay software-visible in the chipset or in a video card. What adapts the core to MiSTer, such as the 15 kHz conversion, the output mixer and the scaler, sits after the native raster. Choosing a different output never changes what a program measures.

Project direction

What this core does and does not claim

Explicit goals

  • Recreate the IBM PC/XT, the Tandy 1000 and the IBM PCjr as usable machines on MiSTer.
  • Share one CPU, chipset and memory system, so a fix reaches every machine.
  • Keep each video card understandable and testable on its own.
  • Make CGA composite useful on CRT and LCD, and 350-line modes usable on 15 kHz televisions.
  • Keep the BIOS, OSD, splash and credits recoverable from the selected output.

Outside the current goal

  • VGA, SVGA or VBE: the EGA card is strictly an EGA.
  • AT-class machines and the 80286.
  • Every model and peripheral of the period.
  • Guaranteeing every BIOS, demo, disk image or CRT timing combination.
  • Claiming that Max has the timing of a 4.77 MHz motherboard.
